# European EV Fast-Charging Networks: Operator Scale, Per-kWh Pricing and Expansion Activity

## Executive Summary

IONITY, Electra and Fastned together operate nearly 12,000 rapid and ultra-rapid chargers in Western Europe. EnBW reports more than 8,000 fast-charging points in Germany and plans roughly 20,000 by 2030. Ultra-fast, no-subscription prices at the charger are about €0.59 per kWh in Spain and €0.60 in France, against €0.78 to €0.80 in Belgium, Switzerland and Italy. Ionity's subscription price is €0.31 per kWh in France and €0.41 in Germany. Eleport is to add more than 250 ultra-fast hubs in eight countries under a €35 million EIB loan. By the end of March 2026, all EU countries except Malta had enough public charging capacity for the BEVs on the road.

## Fast-charging prices per kWh

**Confidence: MEDIUM**

A typical ultra-fast kWh at the charger with no subscription costs about €0.59 in Spain and €0.60 in France, versus €0.78 to €0.80 in Belgium, Switzerland and Italy. [Source: drivethecontinent.com, 2026-09-30, credibility: 0.66] Ionity's subscription prices per kWh are €0.31 in France, €0.41 in Germany, €0.39 in Austria, CHF 0.46 in Switzerland, €0.48 in Italy, €0.37 in Spain, €0.43 in Belgium and €0.43 in the Netherlands. [Source: drivethecontinent.com, 2026-09-30, credibility: 0.66] Ionity's subscription prices per kWh are £0.47 in the United Kingdom, €0.46 in Ireland, DKK 2.20 in Denmark, SEK 3.47 in Sweden and NOK 3.00 in Norway. [Source: drivethecontinent.com, 2026-09-30, credibility: 0.66]

Ad-hoc fast-charging in France ranges from 0.45 to 0.70 €/kWh, and Allego's direct-pay tariff there is 0.52-0.79 €/kWh. [Source: travelinformation.eu, 2026-09-29, credibility: 0.66] A Tesla Supercharging Membership in France costs 9.99 €/month and brings non-Tesla per-kWh pricing close to Tesla-owner pricing at Superchargers. ## EU charging coverage and AFIR targets

**Confidence: LOW**

AFIR requires DC fast chargers of 150 kW or more to be installed every 60 km along the TEN-T Core and Comprehensive networks by 2025. [Source: transportenvironment.org, 2026-07-20, credibility: 0.43] In June 2026, 79% of the TEN-T Core network, mainly national motorways, had met the 2025 AFIR target for ultra-fast chargers. [Source: transportenvironment.org, 2026-07-20, credibility: 0.43] Most remaining gaps in AFIR Core network coverage are in Eastern EU countries and Spain, which also have the fastest growing networks of ultra-fast chargers. [Source: transportenvironment.org, 2026-07-20, credibility: 0.43]

For the TEN-T Comprehensive network, 20 out of 27 EU countries have already achieved their 2027 AFIR target, 18 months ahead of the deadline. [Source: transportenvironment.org, 2026-07-20, credibility: 0.43] AFIR requires member states to have at least 1.3 kW of public charging capacity for every battery electric vehicle in the national fleet. [Source: transportenvironment.org, 2026-07-20, credibility: 0.43] By the end of March 2026, all EU countries except Malta had enough public charging capacity for the number of BEVs on the road. [Source: transportenvironment.org, 2026-07-20, credibility: 0.43]

When all national targets are aggregated, the EU as a whole would exceed the AFIR fleet-based target by 180%. [Source: transportenvironment.org, 2026-07-20, credibility: 0.43] The AFIR Delegated Acts introduced in 2025 set new charging-station density targets and mandatory data-sharing requirements for charge point operators. [Source: ampeco.com, 2026-10-17, credibility: 0.47] AFIR requires new public charging stations of 50 kW or greater to offer ad-hoc payment by debit card, credit card and smartphone directly at the station. [Source: driivz.com, 2026-02-08, credibility: 0.36]

The German government aims to install one million public charging points by 2030. [Source: marketdataforecast.com, 2026-06-20, credibility: 0.61]

## Gaps & Limitations

- No ranking of operators by number of high-power chargers: the evidence gives a combined figure for IONITY, Electra and Fastned but no individual counts for each of them.
- Allego's size differs by publisher (more than 34,000 charge points per RAC; more than 35,000 per EV Magazine), and neither figure is a high-power count.
- Tesla's Supercharger total (more than 75,000) is not broken down for Europe.
- Spain's fast-charging price differs by publisher (about €0.59 per kWh per Drive the Continent; median €0.47 per Eleport) and the difference is unresolved.
- Hungarian pricing is unresolved: Drive the Continent says only Ionity publishes a readable price at source, while Eleport lists MOL Plugee, Shell Recharge and OMV prices.
- Pricing evidence for most operators (Electra, Fastned, EnBW) is missing, and there is no single typical per-kWh figure for Europe; one pricing source before 2025-07-27 was excluded as outside the last 12 months.
- Expansion evidence covers only Eleport's EIB-backed hubs and bk World lounges with partners; no announced charger-count targets in the last 12 months from IONITY, Fastned, Electra, Allego or Tesla, and EnBW's 2030 target has no stated announcement date.
- One transportenvironment.org source before 2024-12-14 was excluded from the AFIR coverage research as outside the 18-month window.
- 1 statement(s) were removed because the gathered evidence did not support them.
